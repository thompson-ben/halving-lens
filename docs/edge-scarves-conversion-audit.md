# Edge Scarves (edgescarves.com) — Conversion & Trust Audit

**Prepared:** 21 July 2026
**Lens:** Preparing the site for £20,000/month Meta + Google Ads spend, targeting UK women aged 40–70 buying for themselves or as gifts.
**Positioning under test:** "The thoughtful gift, made easy."

---

## Methodology & honesty statement

This audit was produced in an environment where **direct browsing of edgescarves.com was blocked at the network level**. Every finding below is built from search-engine index data: page titles, meta descriptions, indexed URLs, product snippets, cached Trustpilot pages, wholesale marketplace listings, Companies House search, and competitor SERP data. That channel is surprisingly rich — it exposes exactly what Google sees, which is exactly what your paid and organic visitors' first impressions are built from.

Two consequences:

1. **Everything quoted below is real** — actual URLs, actual title tags, actual product names, actual review wording, actual prices. Nothing is invented.
2. **Anything I could not verify by direct viewing is explicitly marked ◐ UNVERIFIED** — chiefly visual design, layout, mobile rendering, page speed, and on-page apps. For those sections I give you the evidence-based inference plus the exact checklist to run yourself in 20 minutes.

An audit that pretended to have seen your hero image would be worthless. This one tells you what the data proves — and the data alone is enough to find serious problems.

## 🚨 Critical Revenue Blockers

**These would stop me spending £20,000/month tomorrow. In order of severity:**

### Blocker 1 — The maths doesn't work at your AOV. (Fatal until fixed)
Your scarves retail at roughly £19–£29 (search index shows the range "$18.00 to $37.00" USD-converted across `/collections/all`; top scarf ~$37, most items $24–$34). Assume a generous £28 AOV. At a realistic 1–1.5% cold-traffic conversion rate and ~£0.70–£1.00 blended CPC for UK women's fashion on Meta/Google in 2026, your cost per acquisition lands at **£50–£90 against a £28 basket**. Even at a 3% conversion rate you're near break-even before product cost, wrapping, and £3.49-subsidised shipping. £20k/month at these numbers is a machine for converting cash into beautifully wrapped losses. **Nothing else in this audit matters until AOV strategy (bundles, multi-buy, threshold nudges, gift add-ons) and conversion rate are fixed together.** Your existing "HAT, SCARF & GLOVES ACCESSORY BUNDLE" line and the £50 free-shipping threshold are the right instincts — they need to become the centre of the strategy, not the edge of it.

### Blocker 2 — You'd be paying to advertise sold-out stock.
Search snippets show "Sale Sold out" plastered across the catalogue, **including the Best Sellers collection itself**: "Hearts Scarf | Purple", "Wave Scarf | Pink & Purple", "Floral Scarf | Pink" — all flagged sold out on `/collections/best-sellers`. Also sold out: Summer Floral Scarf, Eco Style Hearts, multiple Allie Heavyweight colourways, the Ginko print, "Animal Print Warm Tassel Scarf – Grey", "Magenta & Blue Mix Tartan Scarf". Products show "only 1 left in stock" / "only 2 left in stock" (Summer Scarf | Peony Blue, Summer Floral Scarf). A best-sellers page that is a graveyard of unavailable items is the single fastest way to burn ad money: the ad promises, the site can't deliver, the visitor leaves with a worse impression than if they'd never clicked. **Paid traffic requires inventory depth on hero products or dynamic exclusion of out-of-stock items from ads and collections.**

### Blocker 3 — The "exclusive" claim is provably false, and the products are traceable wholesale stock.
Your About Us page says you sell "beautiful and exclusive ladies scarves **that can't be found anywhere else**." The search index says otherwise:
- The **"Amy Heavyweight Scarf"** is listed on the **Faire wholesale marketplace** (faire.com/product/p_xgnp36pvk2) as a Sarta Accessories product, and is sold at sartaco.com and verystylishgirl.co.uk.
- The **"Eco Style" Repreve line** (your Simply Leopard scarves) is sold by at least three other small UK retailers: tivyhall.co.uk, kittybrown.co.uk, donnasgiftemporium.com.
- Product titles like *"Amy Heavyweight Scarf - Orange, Traditional Check: One-size"* are unedited supplier-feed syntax, visible to every shopper.

A customer aged 40–70 who googles a product name before buying — and gift buyers do — finds the identical scarf elsewhere, possibly cheaper, and your core brand claim collapses. This is also a Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations exposure ("exclusive" as a false material claim). **Fix: reposition honestly around curation + presentation ("hand-picked, beautifully wrapped"), rename supplier-feed titles, and drop "can't be found anywhere else" entirely.**

### Blocker 4 — Trust infrastructure is missing where a 55-year-old gift buyer looks for it.
- **No policy pages are indexed at all**: no `/policies/shipping-policy`, `/policies/refund-policy`, `/policies/privacy-policy`, `/policies/terms-of-service`, no `/pages/delivery`, no `/pages/faq`, no `/pages/contact` appear anywhere in the index. Delivery and returns wording ("FREE standard UK delivery on orders over £50", "£3.49 for orders under £50", "14-day return or exchange policy") surfaces only inside product-page body text.
- **No legal entity is findable.** Companies House shows no company registered as "Edge Scarves"; the site's indexed pages state no registered company name, number, or VAT number. For your demographic — the most fraud-cautious cohort online — an accessories site with no company details, a Gmail-adjacent contact route, and no phone number reads as risk.
- **Trustpilot is your only external proof, and it's decaying**: cached snapshots show the score sliding from **4.6–4.8 "Excellent" at ~112 reviews to 4.2 "Great" at ~138–146 reviews** — recent reviews skew negative — and **no review dated after mid-2025 surfaced anywhere**. A stale, declining review profile is worse than it looks: Trustpilot widgets show the current score, and 4.2 with no recent activity whispers "is this shop still trading?"

### Blocker 5 — "Luxury" is claimed, polyester is delivered.
The homepage title tag promises "**Luxury** Scarves Beautifully Gift Wrapped." The catalogue is: 100% polyester ("Silky Navy Pansy Print Scarf… **silk feel**… made from 100% Polyester"), viscose, recycled REPREVE polyester, one 70/30 viscose-cashmere blend. There is **no silk, no wool, no cashmere-led product** in the indexed range. One Trustpilot reviewer already caught this: a May 2025 review states a scarf was *"advertised as cotton but was actually polyester… very disappointing."* The word "luxury" sets an expectation the fabric can't meet, and it manufactures your own negative reviews. **"Beautifully gift-wrapped", "hand-picked", "vibrant" — earned. "Luxury" — not yet.** Either buy into a genuine silk/wool/cashmere tier or change the language.
